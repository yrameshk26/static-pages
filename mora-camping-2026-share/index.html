Camping at Samuel de Champlain

A long weekend at a provincial park in Ontario, with a group of families and young kids. The site we used, what we did there, what to bring, and a few things that make a group camp run smoothly.

The trip at a glance

Samuel de Champlain Provincial Park

Site 2 · Fri Jul 31 to Mon Aug 3 · check-in 2:00 pm, check-out 2:00 pm

A four-day, three-night drive-in camping trip. We treated Friday afternoon as set-up and a first wander, Saturday and Sunday as the full camp days, and Monday as a last hike and a 2 pm departure. If you are going with several families, a shared kitchen tent in a central, watery spot is the thing that holds the weekend together.

Where we stayed

Site 2, Samuel de Champlain

3 nights

A regular campsite in the park, used as a cluster of family tents around a common kitchen. Set the kitchen near water and in a central spot, and decide that when you book. Changing it on arrival day is messy. Each family still sets up, tears down and cleans its own site, so budget time for that and do not let it clash with the shared kitchen work.

What to do there

In the park

The days are intentionally loose. Once breakfast is done, the mornings are for swimming, the beach, a canoe, or just staying at the site. Afternoons are a good window for a hike. Evenings are the social bit: tea and cake around 5, supper, then a campfire with marshmallow s'mores for the kids, and songs and games after that.

A simple daily rhythm

Breakfast and lunch prep run early, 6:30 to 9:30 am, so the rest of the morning is free. Lunch is a longer sit, 1 to 3 pm, then a hike, then the fire. It works well with young kids because nobody is rushing between paid attractions, and the little ones can eat a milder version of the same meal.

Food

We cooked as a group, mostly Sri Lankan-style camp food, with a lot of it pre-cooked at home and only reheated on site. Breakfast was tea, egg fry, dhal curry and warmed bread, with tin fish on one of the mornings. Lunch was rice with a heated curry and a fresh eggplant curry, and a milder pot for the kids, chicken one day and mutton or egg the next. Dinners were heated mutton with frozen rotti one night, and shrimp curry with fresh noodles the next.

The useful trick is to freeze the curries ahead of time, bring a big cooler with ice, and keep the on-site cooking to rice, eggs, a fresh vegetable curry and the noodles. Cake for evening tea, watermelon and pineapple, and s'mores at the fire, and nobody has to invent a menu in the dark.

What to bring

Personal camping gear

Tent and a footprint or ground tarp, an extra tarp for rain or shade, sleeping bags and pads, pillows, camp chairs, a flashlight or headlamp, a lantern, a rain jacket, a warm layer for the night, swimwear and towels, toiletries, sunscreen, a basic first aid kit, a phone charger and a small personal cooler. If you have young kids, diapers, wipes and snacks as needed.

If you are camping as a group

Plan a shared kitchen: a gazebo or kitchen tent, big tarps, a couple of propane burners, coolers with ice, rice cookers or an Instant Pot, curry pots, pans, cutting boards, lights, a long outdoor extension cord, buckets, and plenty of paper plates, cups, foil and garbage bags. Whoever brings a piece of kit takes it back home. Bring your own water bottles and any extra snacks or drinks that are not part of the common supply.

Practical tips

  • Arrive between 2 and 6 pm on Friday. That is when tents go up and the common kitchen can be standing in time to store the shared food and kit. One person from each family should help with Friday set-up or Monday teardown.
  • Pre-cook the heavy curries at home. Heating is much easier than cooking a full meal for a crowd on a burner, and you can keep a milder pot for young kids.
  • Mosquitoes and fire. A Thermacell or similar, a fire starter, and marshmallows will get more use than you think.
  • Keep receipts if you are splitting food and fuel costs at the end. A shared sheet is enough.
  • Monday is a pack-down morning. Breakfast, take the kitchen tent and tarps down, a last hike if there is time, and be out by 2 pm.
