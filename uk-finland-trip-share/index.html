Winter Expedition

UK and Finland, with a night in Dublin

Our route, the hotels we picked, and what we would tell another family to do. Written mid-trip, so the days still ahead are the plan, not a review.

Where we stayed

The BoTree London

1 night

Marylebone

A one-night landing pad, eight minutes from Bond Street. The farmers market and Regent's Park are a short, flat walk. Rooms are not ready until mid-afternoon, so leave the bags and plan a nap once you can check in.

The Stratford, Autograph Collection

4 nights

Stratford, London

The working London base. Step-free Elizabeth and Jubilee lines, and Westfield Stratford City is right there for an easy dinner with young kids. This is the hotel we would pick again if the trip started in London for a few days.

The Caledonian

3 nights

Princes Street, Edinburgh

Lined up for the location: the Christmas market is under the castle, a short walk from the door. After a morning train with kids, the first afternoon is really for settling in.

Hilton Helsinki Airport

2 nights, then 1 more

Indoor walkway to the terminal

The practical reason for an airport hotel: late arrivals and early departures with no taxi, a Finnish buffet breakfast, a kids menu downstairs at Restaurant Gui, and a sauna. We also used it to leave a city bag while we were in Lapland, then picked it up on the way back.

Aurora Pyramid Glass Igloos

1 night

Levi, Lapland

The special night: outdoor hot tub and a chance at the aurora. Confirm a late check-in, because the drive from Rovaniemi arrives after dark. One night is enough, then move into the village.

Levi Suites Levin Klubi

3 nights

Levi village centre

The snow-week base we lined up for drying cabinets, which matter after husky and snowmobile days, and for being in the village by the gondola, the spa and the grocery stores.

Lomavekarit Apartments

1 night

Near Rovaniemi Airport

A simple overnight after SantaPark, close enough that the morning car drop-off is about ten minutes.

Hilton Dublin Airport

1 night

A short hop from Terminal 2

Picked for the early US Preclearance morning. Ask for early check-in so the kids can nap after a dawn flight from Helsinki. Shuttle or a five minute taxi from the terminal.

Highlights and what we would recommend

London

  • Tower of London and an Uber Boat down the Thames

    The Crown Jewels are the paid part, about £38 per adult, under-5s free. Tickets are non-refundable, and online is cheaper than the gate. The exterior is free. Then take the Thames Clipper from Tower Pier to Westminster. Contactless at the pier, and a stroller rolls straight on. Big Ben and Parliament are right there from the riverside. hrp.org.uk

  • Madame Tussauds

    We did this. Book the first morning slot online, cheaper than the door, and the queue is shortest before eleven. Two minutes from Baker Street, a straight Jubilee ride from Stratford. The taxi ride and the Marvel floor were the parts that landed with a young child. Under 2s are free, but still add a free ticket. About £27 to £29 per adult and £24 per child aged 2 to 15 online. madametussauds.com

  • Afternoon tea, then Hyde Park Winter Wonderland

    Royal Lancaster London sits on the north edge of Hyde Park, about a ten minute walk to the Marble Arch gate. Winter Wonderland is timed entry, so arrive inside your slot. Rides can be bought on the day. The Santa Land Unlimited Ride Pass is about £25, cheaper online, and it suits a 4 year old. hydeparkwinterwonderland.com

  • Changing the Guard and the Natural History Museum

    The ceremony is free, about 45 minutes, and only runs on certain days, so check the date. It can be called off in heavy rain. Get there early for a view from the Victoria Memorial steps. Then South Kensington for the Natural History Museum. Head straight for the dinosaurs. If it pours, the Science Museum basement has The Garden, free water and construction play for under 6s.

  • Borough Market, Horizon 22 and the Southbank market

    Go to the market early, it backs up from noon. Horizon 22 at 22 Bishopsgate is free, 254m, and the highest free viewpoint in Europe, but you need a timed slot. Keep about 20 minutes to walk there from the market. Then a flat, stroller-friendly river walk to the Southbank Centre Winter Market, which is free to wander. Rain backup: Young V&A in Bethnal Green, about ten minutes from Stratford, built for under 14s.

  • A gentle first day in Marylebone

    If you also land at breakfast, keep day one light. The Sunday farmers market in the Cramer Street car park is two minutes from The BoTree, and Marylebone Green in Regent's Park is the closest playground to the Baker Street gate. Protect the afternoon nap. A long first day with young kids is won or lost there.

Edinburgh

  • National Museum of Scotland

    Free, no ticket, and big enough to swallow a wet morning. Hands-on galleries that work for small children, plus a roof terrace over the Old Town. nms.ac.uk

  • Christmas at the Botanics, and the Princes Street market

    The Princes Street Christmas Market is free to enter, with rides you pay as you go, and it sits under the castle. Christmas at the Botanics is the one to book early: an illuminated trail, timed entry, stroller friendly, with one steep incline. From £19.50 off-peak and £26 standard. Saturday is peak and sells out first. rbge.org.uk

  • Palace of Holyroodhouse, and a short hill walk

    £22 per adult in advance, under-5s free. rct.uk For a walk, keep to the base of Arthur's Seat or Calton Hill if you have a stroller.

Helsinki

  • Children's Town and the Senate Square market

    The Ring Rail train from the airport to Helsinki Central takes about 30 minutes, and is free if someone is pushing a stroller. Children's Town at the City Museum is on Senate Square, and the Christmas market outside has a vintage wooden carousel. On the way back through Helsinki, a walk along the Esplanadi for the lights is enough if everyone is tired.

Lapland

  • Husky, snowmobile and a shot at the aurora

    We lined up a 5 km self-drive husky safari, about 2.5 hours including kitting up, with kids riding wrapped in the sled. A family snowmobile safari works if one adult drives and the other stays with the kids for sledding or the spa. The aurora tour is an evening handover, and pick-up is included, so nobody has to drive afterwards. Book these early in peak winter.

  • Levi gondola, Water World and Samiland

    Daylight is short, so get outside first. The gondola is stroller friendly and under 6s ride free. Levi Hotel Spa has kids pools, slides and the Ailu play area, walk in, no booking. Samiland, by the gondola, is a warm indoor stop with reindeer feeding outside.

  • SantaPark and Santa Claus Village

    SantaPark is a heated underground cavern, which makes it the reliable option whatever the weather does. All-day access, and the 1 year old is free. Santa Claus Village, on the Arctic Circle, is free to enter, with individual activities priced separately. Do the indoor park first, then the village.

Dublin

  • A short city day, then TwinkleTown

    Pick one daytime area, not both. The default is the centre: Trinity College front square is free to walk, the Book of Kells is optional and quiet, then Grafton Street lights down to St Stephen's Green, which has a duck pond and a playground. In the late afternoon, TwinkleTown at Smithfield Square is better once the lights are on. Free entry, kids rides and an ice rink cost extra, and you can eat from the stalls. If the kids are wiped out, Newbridge House and Farm is about 20 minutes from the airport hotel, or Howth for a harbour walk and fish and chips if it is dry. Phoenix Park is the free, closer alternative, with a wild deer herd and a playground.

Food picks

  • Borough Market, and Padella

    Graze early: Bread Ahead doughnuts, toasties and the hot food stalls. Or queue at Padella, next to the market, for fresh pasta. The line is shortest just before opening.

  • Dishoom, Flat Iron or Hawksmoor

    An early dinner works with young kids. Dishoom, Covent Garden or Shoreditch, is Bombay cafe food that kids like. Flat Iron is the easy, cheaper steak. Hawksmoor if you want the big steak night. Book, or go before six.

  • Afternoon tea at Royal Lancaster London

    A sit-down pause that also lands you next to Winter Wonderland. Book a table for a family of 4.

  • A Scottish Sunday roast

    Reserve a family table in a historic pub after Holyroodhouse.

  • Restaurant Gui, Hilton Helsinki Airport

    Downstairs at the hotel, with a kids menu. Useful on the night you get back from Lapland.

  • TwinkleTown stalls, or fish and chips in Howth

    Dinner from the market if you stay in the city, or a classic harbour supper if you take the Howth backup.

Practical tips

  • Getting around London. The Elizabeth Line is step-free from Heathrow to Bond Street and on to Stratford. The Jubilee line runs Stratford to Baker Street with no changes, about 25 minutes, and Stratford to London Bridge in about 15. Contactless works on Tube, train and the Uber Boat.
  • Travel with young kids. Land, dump bags, then protect the first nap. Pre-feed before a red-eye so the meal service is not the first thing they eat, and put pajamas on after takeoff. For the snow week, two toddler car seats in the rental SUV matter, as do drying cabinets at the village hotel.
  • What to book early. Christmas at the Botanics on a Saturday, Winter Wonderland timed entry, Horizon 22 (free, but slotted), the Tower, Madame Tussauds first thing, Holyroodhouse, afternoon tea, the Natural History Museum, and the Lapland safaris plus SantaPark. Changing the Guard only runs on certain days, so fix the London dates around one that does.
  • Helsinki and Lapland. Leave a city bag at the airport hotel on the way north and collect it on the way back, no valuables and no lithium batteries. Check bag rules on the short Lapland hops, they can be a separate ticket. Pick up a rental SUV at Rovaniemi with studded winter tires, then take the drive to Levi slowly. Groceries can wait until the village, or stop at the Prisma near the airport.
  • The way home. Dublin's US Preclearance is why the Chicago connection works. Be at Terminal 2 three hours early. Once you clear in Dublin you arrive in Chicago as a domestic flight, with no immigration queue and no bags to reclaim.
